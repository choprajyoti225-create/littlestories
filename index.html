FICTION · A LITTLE HOPE1 MIN READ

Every evening, long after the last fishing boat had returned, Mara climbed the lighthouse stairs and lit the lamp. The village said there was no need anymore; the ships had satellites, and the sea had grown quiet. But her father had once told her that a light was not only for the lost—it was for anyone who needed to know someone was waiting. So she kept it burning. One winter night, a letter arrived from a stranger across the bay: “I see your light from my window. On the hardest nights, it reminds me to stay.” Mara folded the letter, tucked it beside the lamp, and climbed the stairs again.

Sometimes, hope is just a light left on.✳
